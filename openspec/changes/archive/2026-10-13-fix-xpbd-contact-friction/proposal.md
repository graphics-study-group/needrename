## Why

The XPBD contact solver's friction model does not behave like Coulomb friction: its strength depends on body mass and on `num_velocity_iters` rather than on the friction coefficients alone, and the position phase applies no tangential resistance at all, so nothing can ever stick. `RigidBodyStaticFriction` is uploaded to the GPU but bound by no shader, so the authored static coefficient is dead data. Four further defects sit in the same code path and are cheap to fix while it is open: kinematic bodies are not treated as immovable in the effective-mass denominators of *all four* constraint accumulate shaders, the velocity phase applies a normal correction to contacts the position phase never solved, the contact index is not bounded by the contact-point capacity (which lets an over-budget collision count write past the contact multiplier buffer, and the detector publishes such a count because it reserves slots for points it then drops), and a spinning kinematic body is still integrated by the Coriolis term.

## What Changes

- **Velocity phase friction is solved per direction.** The normal and tangential parts of a contact are separate constraint directions with different effective masses (`w(n) != w(t)` whenever the contact has a lever arm), so each is converted from a target velocity into an impulse with its own inverse effective mass instead of one blended direction.
- **The Coulomb cap is expressed in impulse space.** `p_n = |lambda_n| / dt` is an impulse; the tangential impulse is clamped by `mu_d * p_n` and converted to a velocity correction with the tangential effective mass. The current code compares the impulse cap against a velocity, which scales the bound by the reduced mass (about x2 at 1 kg, x50 at 100 kg, /200 at 0.01 kg).
- **Friction budgets are accumulated per substep.** The tangential impulse a contact has already been given is tracked across all iterations of its phase, so the Coulomb bound holds for the substep rather than per iteration (today the velocity bound is re-granted every velocity iteration, up to `num_velocity_iters` times the physical bound).
- **The position phase gains static friction, measured over the whole substep.** The pin uses the *substep-start* pose of the two contact material points (the buffers `SubstepStartPosition` / `SubstepStartOrientation` already exist and are snapshotted before force integration), because the collision detector re-anchors its paired contact points to the post-integration geometry — their tangential offset is exactly zero at detection, so a detection-time reference would measure nothing and would leave the substep's integrated sliding displacement uncorrected (a steady slope creep of `g * sin(theta) * h^2` per substep, about 2.8 cm/s on a 20 degree slope at h = 1/120 s). The tangential drift is resisted by a constraint clamped so that the accumulated tangential multiplier stays within `mu_s * lambda_n`; a correction that does not fit the remaining budget is skipped, and the velocity phase's kinetic friction owns that contact.
- **Stick and slip are partitioned so the two phases never charge friction twice for the same motion.** The position phase's static correction cancels the drift, which makes the pose-derived tangential velocity zero, so the velocity phase — which is applied unconditionally, matching the reference XPBD scheme — has nothing left to remove. When the static constraint is skipped, the velocity phase's kinetic friction with `mu_d` is the only tangential resistance, which is exactly the sliding case.
- **`RigidBodyStaticFriction` is bound** by the position phase (the buffer already exists and is populated).
- **Kinematic bodies are immovable in constraint solving.** `PhysicsScene::SubmitRigidBody` writes zero mass and zero inverse inertia into the solver-facing SoA mirror for a kinematic body (authored values stay in the descriptor and component), and each of the four constraint accumulate shaders additionally treats a kinematic body by flag, so a kinematic platform no longer softens the contacts and joints attached to it.
- **The velocity phase's normal correction is gated on `lambda_n > 0`**, so contacts the position phase never solved (speculative points inside the contact margin, already-separated contacts) no longer have their normal velocity cancelled.
- **Contact indices are bounded by the contact-point capacity** in both contact accumulate shaders, and **`collision_count` never exceeds the configured contact budget**: the detection shader hands out slot ranges with an allocation cursor that may overshoot, and derives the published count from the ranges actually taken with one monotone atomic, so the bound holds under any interleaving without a retry loop.
- **The contact-point capacity has one owner.** It is derived once, by the solver, and passed into the narrow-phase detector, which uses it as both its result-buffer length and its write budget instead of deriving a second value from the broad-phase pair count.
- **Atomic updates complete in one instruction.** A new cross-cutting requirement forbids obtaining an atomic update by repeated attempts, so no kernel's progress depends on contention and no invocation's contribution is dropped by an exhausted attempt budget.
- **Kinematic bodies skip angular integration entirely.** The Coriolis term is currently subtracted after the torque is zeroed, so a kinematic body spinning about a non-principal axis precesses as if it were free.
- **The contact normal's direction wording is corrected** in `mpr.glsl`, `perturbation.glsl` and the solver spec: the normal points from body A toward body B (it is the CSO face normal pointing away from the interior point), which is what makes `dot(pt_b - pt_a, n) < 0` for overlap and `C > 0` a penetration test.
- **BREAKING (internal)**: the position and velocity accumulate shaders each declare additional bindings (appended, to keep the 0..N consecutive-binding contract), the position shader re-declares the two substep-start buffers that were removed from it in an earlier change, the detection shader declares a `ContactCursor` binding and the detector owns the matching buffer, and the solver owns two new per-contact accumulator buffers. The solver-facing mass and inverse-inertia columns of a kinematic body become zero. `ConvexCollisionDetector::BindToScene` takes a contact capacity instead of a `max_contact_points` hint, `CollisionResultBuffers::max_output_collision_pairs` becomes `contact_capacity`, and both collision detectors become exported symbols of `EnginePhysics`.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `gpu-kernel-atomics`: an atomic update is a single instruction and never a retry loop, and a kernel that must both allocate index ranges and publish a bounded value carries those two roles in two counters.

### Modified Capabilities

- `xpbd-contact-solve`: the position phase gains a static friction step measured over the whole substep and kinematic bodies become immovable in every constraint denominator; the velocity phase's friction model, its per-substep budgeting, its unconditional kinetic correction and its normal-correction gate are specified; both contact accumulate shaders get an index bound; the multiplier-lifetime requirement covers the two new per-substep accumulators; the integration requirement states that kinematic bodies skip the angular update.
- `gpu-convex-collision-detection`: the collision count is defined as the number of points actually written, so it can never exceed the configured contact-write budget; the contact capacity becomes a caller-owned value the detector sizes its buffers to and enforces as its write budget; the reservation mechanism is specified as an allocation cursor plus a published bound.
- `physics-dll-module`: `SpatialHashBroadDetector` and `ConvexCollisionDetector` join the exported class list, because the published contact count and the contact capacity are detector-owned state that no exported solver API exposes, so the count's bound could otherwise not be asserted by a test.
- `com-descriptors`: `PhysicsScene::SubmitRigidBody` writes zero mass and inverse inertia to the SoA mirror for a kinematic body instead of the descriptor's values.

## Impact

- **Shaders**: `solver/XPBDSolver/accumulate_contact_position.comp` (substep-start friction drift, `RigidBodyStaticFriction`, tangential accumulator, kinematic inverse mass, index bound), `solver/XPBDSolver/accumulate_contact_velocity.comp` (per-direction masses, impulse-space cap, per-substep budget, kinematic inverse mass, index bound), `solver/XPBDSolver/accumulate_hinge_position.comp` and `accumulate_fixed_position.comp` (kinematic denominators), `solver/XPBDSolver/integrate_forces.comp` (kinematic angular skip), `collision/ConvexCollisionDetector/detect_collisions.comp` (count reservation as a cursor plus a published bound), plus comment-only corrections in `collision/ConvexCollisionDetector/mpr.glsl` and `perturbation.glsl`.
- **Host**: `engine/Physics/Solver/XPBDGpuSolver.{h,cpp}` — two new solver-owned buffers, their per-substep clears, the new descriptor bindings, the re-binding of the two substep-start snapshots into the position accumulate dispatch, and passing the solver-owned contact capacity to the narrow detector; `engine/Physics/Collision/ConvexCollisionDetector.{h,cpp}` — the capacity parameter, the detector-owned cursor buffer, the removal of the detector's own capacity derivation, and `PHYSICS_API`; `engine/Physics/Collision/SpatialHashBroadDetector.h` — `PHYSICS_API`; `engine/Physics/PhysicsScene.cpp` — the kinematic mirror rule. The only public-interface change is the two detectors becoming exported; `XpbdGpuSolver`'s own interface is unchanged.
- **Tests**: two new headless tests (a direct `XpbdGpuSolver` fixture for the mass / iteration / direction invariances and a `PhysicsApp` `PhysicsOnly` test for slope hold and creep), both registered without `WINDOWED`.
- **Behavior**: `static_friction` becomes effective, so scenes that authored a static coefficient different from the dynamic one will slide/stick differently; a body that previously crept down a slope now holds; friction strength stops varying with body mass and with `num_velocity_iters`; kinematic partners stop softening contacts and joints.
- **No change** to the collision detection algorithm, the entry/sort/reduce pipeline, the scratch layout, the push-constant blocks, or the shader file list.
